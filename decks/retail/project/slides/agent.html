The detector and the assistant help people decide. An agent decides, so we gave Store 014 one and ran June again. The agent is scripted, a few rules standing in for a language model’s decisions, so every run can be repeated. At ten every night, before the store’s ordering run, it reviews the seven warehouse items. It can change an item’s forecast for a week, or ask staff to check a shelf at eight the next morning, and it has one rule: if an item’s demand over three days came to less than half its forecast while the item was available, demand has dropped, so cut the forecast. The first version reads the KPIs as reported. On Thursday night it saw 34 bags sold in three days against a forecast of 74, with availability at 100% on the books, and cut the forecast. The shelf had been empty since Wednesday, with the cases behind the rice; the cut did nothing for it. The second version reads censored hours. On Wednesday night it saw six open hours without a sale while the books showed 56 bags, asked for a check, and at eight on Thursday staff found the bags in the wrong bay. Five kilo atta lost 157 bags with no agent, 137 with the first version, by luck, and 51 with the second. Over forty more Junes the averages are 221, 214 and 105, and the second version beat no agent in 39 of the forty, with no more stock. Same rule, same tools. The second knew what the records cannot see, and had a way to go and look.
