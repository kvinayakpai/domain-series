The first change models how the data is observed. A gap detector learns each item’s usual hourly sales from May’s till lines. It adds up the sales it expects over every run of open hours with no sales while the books show stock, and raises an alert once a zero that long is less likely than one in a thousand. On June’s records it flags five kilo atta at 19:00 on Wednesday, three open hours after the shelf emptied. Across the seven warehouse items it raises 10 alerts; 6 fall in real gaps, and it finds 6 of the 10 gaps that lasted three open hours or more. Rerun June with someone checking the shelf within an hour of each alert, and five kilo atta loses 35 bags instead of 157. The threshold is a business decision: one in a hundred finds more gaps and sends staff to more full shelves.
