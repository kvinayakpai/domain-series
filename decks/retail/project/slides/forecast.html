The second change unconstrains demand before training. Keep the store’s rule, a 28-day mean, and change its input: fill every censored hour, the runs the detector flagged and the hours the books show no stock, with the sales the detector expected. The forecast holds: 25.6 bags a day at the end of June instead of 20.2, against the 24.9 that shoppers came for; it runs a little high because some shoppers who found the shelf empty came back later and are counted twice. Over June its bias against shoppers falls from −8% to −1%. Now look at the other column: scored on recorded sales, the unconstrained forecast looks worse, +22% instead of +13%. A team that evaluates on recorded sales would reject the better model. The order-up-to level the reorder rule would set rises from 84 bags to 93.
