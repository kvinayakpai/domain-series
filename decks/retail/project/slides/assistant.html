Why did 5 kg atta sales fall? With the domain layer:

From Wed 10 Jun, 15:53 to Sat 13 Jun, 10:43 the tills recorded one bag in 39 open hours, against 68 expected, while the books showed 55–56 bags. The shelf audit at Fri 12 Jun, 11:00 found the bay empty. No delivery arrived before sales resumed, so the bags were in the store but not on the shelf: misplaced or not refilled. With those hours filled in, the week comes to about 155 bags against 217: the empty shelf explains about half of the fall, and the rest is demand easing after a strong first week. Nothing says shoppers changed packs.

The third change gives the assistant the domain. Same assistant, same seven questions, now given a domain layer: definitions, such as sales measure demand only while the item is on the shelf, and book stock is a belief, not an observation; three rules; the tables that observe the shelf, the till lines and shelf audits; and four views built from them: hourly sales, the hourly book, the censored hours and a demand estimate. It answers all seven. Asked why sales fell, it finds the 39 open hours with one bag recorded against 68 expected while the books showed stock, the audit that found the bay empty, and no delivery before sales resumed, so the bags were in the store but not on the shelf; with those hours filled in, the empty shelf explains about half the fall. Published work points the same way: in dbt Labs’ 2026 benchmark, current AI models answered about 65 percent of 11 business questions about an insurance database correctly from the raw tables, and 98 to 100 percent through a semantic layer once three data models had spelled out the business’s terms. Text-to-SQL on the same models reached 84 to 90 percent, and dbt Labs sells the semantic layer, so treat it as direction, not proof.
