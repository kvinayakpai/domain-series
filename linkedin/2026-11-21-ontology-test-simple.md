# LinkedIn post (draft, 21 Nov 2026): the ontology test, simple version

Status: draft, not yet published. For everyone, not only data people.

---

My learning from this experiment – "A KPI is only as good as the meaning behind it, and that meaning has to be written down by someone who knows the business."

So far I have covered 12 KPIs in this series. This is where it is leading.

I ran a small test on one grocery store's records. One product (5 kg atta), one month (June 2026), four questions:

1) How many bags were sold?
2) How many shoppers left without a bag?
3) How often was it on the shelf?
4) What were the lost sales?

What I found –

The records can answer 1 and 3. 567 bags sold, and 6 of 8 shelf checks found it on the shelf (75%).

Nobody records 2 and 4. A shopper who walks away leaves no row in any table.

Still, some AI models gave me a confident number for both, taken from the wrong table. When I wrote down what each table really means (in a few plain rules, like "a sale is not demand"), one model stopped guessing and said "no record holds this". The smallest ones kept guessing.

The lesson – the tables were never the problem. The missing piece is the business meaning, and that is what an ontology is. More on it in the coming posts.
