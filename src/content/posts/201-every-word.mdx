In Season 1 of this series, we looked at eighteen KPIs at Store 014, a supermarket we simulated in Jayanagar, Bengaluru. Every KPI was computed correctly. Every one of them still missed something that actually happened.

When I went back over the season, I noticed something I had not planned for. Under every blind spot there was a word.

Let us look at three of them.

- On-shelf availability for 5 kg atta read 97%. Observed on the shelf, it was 77%. The word was **available** – on the books, or on the shelf?
- On time, in full for June's deliveries read 92%. With every case right, it was 84%. The word was **in full** – the right number of cases, or the right item inside them?
- UPI payment success one Saturday evening read 61% at the till and 82% at the store's bank. The word was **success** – as the till saw it, or as the bank did?

Same word, two meanings, two numbers. Nobody was lying. Each team used the word the way its own system needed it.

## Why does this matter more now?

When a store manager reads "available 97%", the manager may still walk to the shelf and look. An AI agent will not. It reads the word literally and acts on it. If the word means one thing in the warehouse and another on the shop floor, the agent will pick one meaning, and it will not tell you which one it picked.

In one of my earlier blogs I had written – **"If you understand the business language, you cross the language barrier."** I believe the same holds for AI. The difference is that a machine cannot pick up the business language over a cup of tea with the store manager. Someone has to write it down for it.

## What is an ontology?

That written-down business language has a name – an ontology. In simple words, it says three things:

1. What exists in the business – a product, a pack, a case, a shipment, a payment.
2. How these connect – a case holds packs, a shipment carries cases, a payment settles a bill.
3. Which rules must hold – one payment gives one debit, and a 5 kg bag is never the same item as a 1 kg pack.

The big platforms have noticed. Microsoft, for example, describes [the ontology in its Fabric IQ](https://learn.microsoft.com/fabric/iq/overview) as entity types, relationships, rules and metrics that ground what AI agents do. But a tool only stores an ontology. Deciding what "available" means at Store 014 is not a software decision. It is domain knowledge.

**Ontology is all about domain knowledge.**

Eighteen short blogs:

- **Three to start** – this one, a simple ladder from a glossary to a knowledge graph, and the evidence that meaning matters more than the model.
- **Twelve words** – three each from the shelf, the truck and the money, and three from an oil field that Season 3 will explore in full.
