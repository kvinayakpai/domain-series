## Saturday, 6:48 in the evening

It is the Saturday evening rush, and the queues at Store 014 are long. A customer's basket comes to ₹1,064.47. She scans the QR code at the till and approves the payment on her phone. Her bank takes the money from her account.

The till waits 30 seconds, hears nothing back, and shows "Payment failed: no response". The cashier asks for another way to pay. She pays by card, and leaves.

87 seconds after she approved the UPI payment, the store's bank confirms the credit. Nobody at the till sees it. She has paid twice. On Tuesday she phones the store, and on the following Sunday, eight days after her shopping, she gets ₹1,064.47 back.

## Five readings of one payment

As in the other posts, nobody here is careless. Each person reads the record in front of them, and each conclusion follows from it.

**The cashier** sees a screen that says the payment failed. With twenty people waiting, the cashier takes another payment, as every cashier does. Between 18:20 and 19:50 the tills gave up on 28 UPI payments, and cashiers asked for another payment 30 times.

**The payments operations lead** sees a dashboard of approvals at the tills. Approvals fell to 69% and 67% of UPI attempts in the 18:00 and 19:00 hours, and were back to normal by eight. The acquiring bank's switch was slow; the incident is closed, and there is nothing for the stores to do.

**The reconciliation analyst** matches the payment provider's UPI credits with the till's approved payments, by amount and time. On Saturday there were 607 credits against 597 approvals, and on Sunday 670 against 663. The 17 credits with no approved payment behind them go into a suspense account until someone claims them.

**Customer support** takes 26 complaints about payments in June, 23 of them "I was charged twice". The routine is to ask for a screenshot, check it against the provider's report, and refund. It takes about five days.

**The finance controller** sees UPI settle with no fees at all in June, and ₹11,302 of refunds. Since 15 October, though, the same bills carry a merchant fee on every UPI payment above ₹2,000.

## What actually happened

A UPI payment at a till passes through several systems. The customer's app sends it, through NPCI's switch, to her bank, which debits her account. It then travels on to the store's bank, the acquirer, which credits the store, and the answer travels back to the till. On Saturday evening the acquirer's UPI switch slowed down. Between 18:20 and 19:50, 40% of UPI attempts at Store 014 got no answer within the till's 30 seconds.

A slow answer is not a failed payment. When the debit has happened and the credit's answer is late, UPI settles the payment afterwards: the merchant's bank either confirms the credit or returns the money. For a payment to a merchant, RBI has required since 2019 that if the customer is debited and the merchant never gets confirmation, the money comes back within five days of the transaction, with ₹100 a day in compensation after that. The money is never lost. For a while it is in two places, and the till is the last to know.

In those 90 minutes Store 014 took 119 bills. Of 82 UPI attempts, the tills gave up on 28. 15 customers paid twice, two of them three times: 17 extra charges, ₹7,825.69 in all. One more customer was debited for a payment the store never received, and their bank returned the money the next day.

Across June, 24 customers paid twice, 26 extra charges worth ₹13,366.06; 9 of them outside the Saturday slowdown, on ordinary days when a till gives up on about one UPI payment in a thousand. Six customers were debited for payments that never reached the store, and their banks returned every rupee within three days. The store refunded double payments only when a customer complained. The median wait was 4.9 days, the longest 7.8, and when June closed four extra charges, ₹2,846.06, were still sitting in suspense.

## This is the normal case

UPI is enormous, and at that scale small rates are large numbers.

- UPI processed 24.07 billion transactions in September 2026, about 802 million a day, according to NPCI's figures.
- NPCI's chief executive put UPI's technical declines at 0.7–0.8% of transactions in November 2024. Applied to September's volume, 0.75% would be about 180 million failed attempts a month.
- Whole-system slowdowns happen too. On 12 April 2025, UPI's success rate fell to about 50% for nearly two hours, the third disruption in a week. NPCI said some banks had flooded its systems with requests to check the status of transactions.
- NPCI's answer was to ration those checks: the first status check on a transaction should come no sooner than 90 seconds after it, and preferably no more than three within two hours. From 16 June 2025 it also cut the time allowed for a payment response from 30 seconds to 15.

So a till cannot simply ask again until it knows. By the rules, the earliest it may ask is a minute and a half after the customer pays, which at a busy till is a long time to stand still.

## One name, four numbers

"Payment success rate" sounds like one number. Ask how payments went at Store 014 and you can get four honest answers.

<PaymentFourWays />

The payments dashboard reports the first: approvals at the till. The acquirer's settlement reflects the second, which includes the late credits. The store's sales report shows the third, because every bill was paid one way or another. Only the fourth is what customers lived through, and no system computes it, because it needs the customer's side of the story. Over a whole month the four numbers sit within a few points of each other. In the 90 minutes that mattered, they spread from 61% to 100%.

## Same store, different cost: the new UPI fee

Until this month, UPI payments to merchants from bank accounts carried no merchant discount rate, the fee a shop pays to accept a payment. From 15 October 2026, payments to a merchant above ₹2,000 carry 0.4%, capped at ₹300; merchants receiving up to ₹1 lakh a month through UPI QR are exempt, and person-to-person payments stay free. The fee is split among the customer's bank, the acquirer, the UPI app and its partner bank.

<MdrTable />

For Store 014 the fee is small next to its sales, about ₹8,000 a month at June's mix. The shape matters more than the size. The fee jumps from nothing to ₹8 at ₹2,001, and the finance team's view of payments now has a cost line it never had to explain.

## What would have helped

The demo lets you try two changes from 1 June, with the same customers, the same baskets and the same banks.

<PolicyTable />

- **Wait for the status check.** When a UPI payment hangs, the till waits for the check NPCI allows at 90 seconds before asking for another payment. Most late credits arrive within that time, so double charges in June fall from 24 customers to 9. The price is paid at the worst moment: during the slowdown the tills spend 99 minutes taking payment instead of 77.
- **Put the bill ID on every UPI request.** The till's QR code carries the bill it is for, so each credit in the provider's report names its bill. This does not stop double charges, but the next morning's match finds every one and refunds it automatically. The median wait for a refund falls from 4.9 days to 0.7, nothing is left in suspense, and complaints fall from 26 to 12.

The cheaper fix is in the back office. A payment that carries a reference to what it is for can always be matched to it; one that does not can be matched only by hand, and only when someone complains.

A till that hears nothing back never learns the payment's reference number, the RRN, so there is nothing to join on. The provider's report has the RRN and a success; the till has a failure and no RRN. The double charge appears only when you match the provider's successes to the till's approvals, find a credit with no approval behind it, and then find the bill by amount and time. Every table is right on its own. Knowing that a "failed" UPI payment is a question, not an answer, is the domain knowledge.

## How to learn this domain

A rough plan for a few evenings a week over about two months:

1. **How UPI moves money.** Learn the parties in a payment: the customer's app and its bank, NPCI's switch, the customer's bank that debits, the merchant's bank that credits, and the provider that serves the merchant. Then read NPCI's circulars on how a payment that times out is settled afterwards, starting with its 2017 note on debit reversals and deemed approval.
2. **Cards, for contrast.** Read Glenbrook's *Payments Systems in the U.S.* for authorisation, clearing and settlement in the card networks and the four-party model. India's systems differ in detail, and the vocabulary is the same.
3. **When payments fail.** Read RBI's 2019 circular on turnaround times and compensation for failed transactions, and NPCI's 2025 circulars on status checks and response times. Note which party owes what, and by when.
4. **Reconciliation.** Learn the keys that join payment records: the RRN, the merchant's own transaction reference, settlement dates. Practise finding credits with no sale behind them and sales with no credit.
5. **Practice.** [Download June's payment records](../data/#payments-records) and find every customer who paid twice before opening the answer key.
