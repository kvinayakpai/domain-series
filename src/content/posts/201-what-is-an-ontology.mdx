"Ontology" sounds like a word from a philosophy class. In data and AI it has a very practical meaning, and I believe it will be one of the most important things a data team builds in the next few years.

In Season 1 of this series, we looked at eighteen KPIs at Store 014, a supermarket we simulated in Jayanagar, Bengaluru. Every KPI was computed correctly, and every one of them missed something that actually happened. Each fix turned out to be a piece of domain knowledge – what a word really means, which record can be trusted, which rule must always hold.

This season is about writing all of that down in one place. That place is called an ontology.

## So what is an ontology?

An ontology is the language of a business, written down so that a machine can use it. In simple words, it says three things:

1. **What exists** – a product, a pack, a shelf, a truck, a bill, a payment.
2. **How these connect** – a case holds packs, a truck carries pallets, a payment pays a bill.
3. **Which rules must hold** – a 5 kg bag is never the same item as a 1 kg pack, and one bill is paid once.

## You already know one

Think of your neighborhood kirana. The shopkeeper knows that the family in flat 302 buys on udhaar and pays at the end of the month. He knows that atta comes in 1 kg and 5 kg packs, and that they are not the same thing on his shelf. He knows that no family's udhaar can cross the limit he has set.

That is an ontology – the things, the links and the rules of his shop. It just lives in his head. A new helper at the counter picks it up over time. An AI agent cannot. It knows only what someone has written down.

## How is it different from what we already have?

Most companies already have pieces of this, and the words get mixed up. Let me put them on one ladder, with the same kirana:

- **Glossary** – what a word means: udhaar means pay at the end of the month.
- **Taxonomy** – what kind of thing it is: atta is a kind of flour.
- **Data model** – how one system stores it: the billing software's tables for items, bills and customers.
- **Semantic layer** – one formula for one number: monthly sales is bills minus returns.
- **Ontology** – the things, links and rules across all of it: a family holds an udhaar account, a bill belongs to one family, and udhaar cannot cross the limit.
- **Knowledge graph** – the ontology filled in with real things: flat 302's account and this month's bills.

Each step adds something. The ontology is the first step that says how the whole business fits together, across systems, with its rules.

## Why do I call it domain knowledge?

A tool can store an ontology. It cannot write one for you. Whether "available" means on the books or on the shelf, or whether a UPI payment that timed out at the till has failed, is not a software decision. It is domain knowledge, and it sits with the people who run the business.

In one of my earlier blogs I had written – **"If you understand the business language, you cross the language barrier."** An ontology is how we hand that business language to a machine.

**Ontology is all about domain knowledge.**
