When a number falls 59% in a week, what is the first question you ask? I believe most of us ask what the shoppers did. [Like-for-like sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-store_sales) compare sales with an earlier period on the same footing: the same stores, leaving out any that opened or closed in between. [Store 014's weekly review](../retail/#monday-9-am-the-weekly-review) applies the same idea to one item, week on week.

## What the dashboard said

On Monday 15 June, [the review opened with 5 kg atta down 59% on the week: 88 bags against 217](../retail/#monday-9-am-the-weekly-review). [The forecast eased, and the replenishment plan followed it](../retail/#monday-9-am-the-weekly-review).

Read as a preference, [5 kg's share of atta sales fell from 40% to 25% in a week](../retail/#five-readings-of-one-week), which looks like shoppers moving to the 1 kg pack.

## What happened

Because I built the store, I know how many shoppers came for a bag: [224 in the first week of June and 148 in the second](../retail/#what-actually-happened). Part of that 34% fall is normal, because [households stock up in the first week of the month](../retail/#what-actually-happened).

The rest of the drop in sales was the shelf. [From Wednesday afternoon to Saturday morning it stood empty, with 56 bags in the wrong bay](../retail/#what-actually-happened). Across the month, [demand was flat: 24.7 bags a day in May and 24.9 in June](../retail/#what-actually-happened).

**Sales fell 59%, and the shoppers who came for the bag fell 34%.**

## Why the number missed it

Recorded sales are what the tills scanned, and [a till cannot know whether the shelf had stock](../retail/technical/#the-records). So a week with an empty shelf looks like a week of falling demand.

The pack mix misleads the same way. [In the hours the 5 kg shelf was stocked, its share was 40% in the first week and 37% in the second](../retail/technical/#the-assistant-with-and-without-a-domain-layer). The share fell only when there was no 5 kg to buy. [Economists who tracked stock in 54 vending machines found that ignoring stock-outs biases demand estimates](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14315).

## How to catch it

Compare periods only over the hours the shelf was stocked, or compare estimates of demand instead of sales. [The store's gap detector marks the hours the shelf was probably empty](../retail/technical/#finding-the-censored-hours), and [filling them with the sales the item usually makes](../retail/technical/#unconstraining-demand) gives a demand series you can compare week on week.

## If an AI agent reads it

Let me summarize the two answers I got from the series' scripted assistant.

Me – Why did 5 kg atta sales fall this week?

Assistant (given only the schema) – [Shoppers moved to other pack sizes](../retail/technical/#the-assistant-with-and-without-a-domain-layer)

Assistant (given the till lines and the shelf audits) – [39 open hours with one bag sold, and an audit that found the bay empty](../retail/technical/#the-assistant-with-and-without-a-domain-layer)

[A scripted replenishment agent reading the same drop cut the forecast](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it), for a shelf that had stock in the wrong bay.

## Try it on your own data

Take last month's biggest week-on-week drop for one item. Check whether the item had a long run of hours with no sales while the system showed stock. If it did, compare the two weeks again without those hours.

So in summary, before you explain a drop by what shoppers did, check whether they could buy.
