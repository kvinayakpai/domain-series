[On-shelf availability](https://www.ecr-community.org/osa/) asks whether a product is there when a shopper looks for it: in the words of the industry group ECR, available "in the place they expect it and at the time they want to purchase it". An empty shelf sends shoppers away: in [a worldwide study of grocery out-of-stocks](https://www.fmi.org/newsroom/news-archive/view/2002/10/14/new-industry-study-explores-root-causes-of-global-out-of-stock-dilemma), 31% of shoppers said they go to another store when an item is missing, and 9% buy nothing at all.

## What the dashboard said

[Store 014](../data/) is the fictional supermarket in Bengaluru that this series simulates, hour by hour, through June 2026. Its dashboard said 5 kg bags of [atta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atta_(flour)), the wheat flour used in India for flatbreads such as rotis, were [available in 97% of the store's open hours](../retail/technical/#availability-five-definitions).

Because the store is simulated, we know where every bag was: a bag was [on the shelf in 77% of those hours](../retail/technical/#availability-five-definitions).

## What happened

On Tuesday 9 June, fourteen cases of 5 kg atta from the morning truck were [put away in the wrong bay, behind the rice](../retail/#what-actually-happened). The shelf [ran out at 15:48 the next day](../retail/#what-actually-happened), while the system still showed 56 bags. Staff [found the cases on Saturday morning](../retail/#what-actually-happened), after the shelf had stood empty for 39 open hours, and in that time [shoppers came for 71 bags](../retail/#what-actually-happened) that were not there.

[It happened again later in the month](../logistics/#what-actually-happened): a pallet labelled 5 kg turned out to hold 1 kg packs, and the 5 kg shelf stood empty for another 51.5 open hours.

## Why the number missed it

The dashboard never looks at the shelf: [it counts an hour as available](../retail/technical/#availability-five-definitions) if the system shows at least one bag in stock. That stock figure, called book stock, is [the last count plus deliveries, minus sales and write-offs](../retail/technical/#tests-that-pass). Nothing in that sum looks at the shelf, so [the bags behind the rice stayed on the books](../retail/#what-actually-happened), and every hour they sat there counted as available.

This is common in real stores. In [a study of nearly 370,000 inventory records from 37 stores](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ormnsc/v54y2008i4p627-641.html), 65% were wrong.

## How to catch it

[Store 014 already had two ways](../retail/#what-domain-knowledge-changes-in-the-data-work) to see the gap.

The first is to look. Staff [check the shelves at 11:00 every Tuesday and Friday](../retail/#what-domain-knowledge-changes-in-the-data-work), and on Friday 12 June the check found the 5 kg bay empty while the system showed 55 bags. [No report joined that check](../retail/#what-domain-knowledge-changes-in-the-data-work) to the dashboard.

The second is to listen to the tills. Shoppers come for [about 25 bags of 5 kg atta a day](../retail/#what-actually-happened), so a long run with no sales while the system shows stock usually means the shelf is empty. [A simple detector built on that idea](../retail/technical/#finding-the-censored-hours) flags the gap three open hours after the shelf emptied, instead of 39, and [researchers have built similar systems](https://uniwacris.uniwa.gr/handle/3000/2145) that spot empty shelves from sales and other store data.

## If an AI agent reads it

[The agents in this series are scripted](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it): a few fixed rules stand in for a language model, so every run can be repeated and scored.

A replenishment agent that [read availability from book stock](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it) saw 5 kg atta 100% available but selling less than half its forecast, decided demand had dropped, and cut the forecast. It [never asked anyone to check the shelf](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it).

The same agent with [one more rule](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it), that in stock means on the shelf, asked for about six shelf checks a month. Across forty simulated Junes, 5 kg atta [lost 105 bags on average instead of 214](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it), without holding more stock.

## Try it on your own data

Pick your ten best-selling products. For each, find the last time a person looked at the shelf, and compare what they saw with what the system said at that hour. If no record shows anyone looking, that is your first finding.
