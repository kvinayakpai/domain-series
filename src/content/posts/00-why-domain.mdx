[Store 014](../data/) is a supermarket in Bengaluru that exists only in a simulation built for this series. Because it is simulated, [we know what really happened](../data/#retail-records) on every shelf, truck and payment in June 2026, and can check each of its numbers against it.

Here are three of its KPIs for June, each measured twice: as the store's systems reported it, and as it really was.

<KpiPairs ids={['osa', 'otif', 'success-rate']} />

In those 90 minutes on Saturday evening, [15 customers paid twice](../charged-twice/).

Each number was computed correctly, and [31 of the store's 32 data tests passed](../kit/#dbt); the other one warned about milk. Each is still wrong about what it claims to measure, because it comes from a record that cannot see that thing: [book stock cannot see the shelf](../on-shelf-availability/#why-the-number-missed-it), [a shipping notice cannot see inside a pallet](../otif/#why-the-number-missed-it), and [a till cannot see the customer's bank](../payment-success/#why-the-number-missed-it).

Knowing what a record cannot see is domain knowledge. This series argues it is the most useful thing a data or AI team can bring to the age of AI agents.

## Why it matters more now

A person who reads a wrong KPI might question it. An AI agent acts on it.

In this series, [a scripted replenishment agent](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it) read Store 014's records every night and adjusted its forecasts. When 5 kg atta stopped selling, [it saw 100% availability, decided demand had dropped and cut the forecast](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it); the shelf had been empty since the previous afternoon. Given one more rule, [that in stock means on the shelf](../on-shelf-availability/#if-an-ai-agent-reads-it), the same agent asked for a shelf check instead, and across forty simulated Junes [5 kg atta lost half as many sales](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it).

Outside this series, the evidence points the same way.

- In [a 2026 benchmark by dbt Labs](https://docs.getdbt.com/blog/semantic-layer-vs-text-to-sql-2026), current AI models answered 65% of business questions about an insurance database correctly from the raw tables, and 98–100% once the business's terms and rules were written down as data models and a semantic layer.
- [MIT's NANDA initiative found that about 95% of organisations saw no measurable return](https://virtualizationreview.com/articles/2025/08/19/mit-report-finds-most-ai-business-investments-fail-reveals-genai-divide.aspx) from generative AI, and traced it to systems that do not adapt to their context or fit the way people work.

## Two numbers for every KPI

The fix this series argues for is small: give every KPI that matters two numbers, what the system reports and what an independent check observes. [The KPI page](../kpis/) does this for all eighteen of the series' KPIs.

Then track how long the gap takes to find. At Store 014, [joining records the store already had found each gap within hours](../who-saw-what/#hours-by-joining-days-by-waiting); waiting for someone to notice took days.

## How the series works

The series has 25 short posts, one idea each, in five parts.

1. **Start here**: this post.
2. **The shelf**: the story, [Sales are not demand](../retail/), then eight KPIs, starting with [on-shelf availability](../on-shelf-availability/).
3. **The truck**: the story, [On time, in full, and wrong](../logistics/), then five KPIs.
4. **The money**: the story, [Failed, but paid](../payments/), then five KPIs.
5. **Putting it together**: [Who saw what](../who-saw-what/), then two posts for practitioners: testing an AI assistant against an answer key, and writing domain tests in dbt.

Every KPI post has the same shape: what the KPI is, what it said at Store 014, why it missed, how to catch it, and the rule to give an AI agent.

Every claim links to its evidence: a Store 014 number to the analysis that computed it, and a real-world fact to its source. [The records and answer keys](../data/) are free to download, so you can check any number yourself.
