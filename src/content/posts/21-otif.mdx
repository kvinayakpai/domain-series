On time, in full (OTIF) is the share of deliveries that arrive when promised with everything ordered, and [there is no standard definition of it](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/defining-on-time-in-full-in-the-consumer-sector): companies differ on the time window, and on whether "in full" is judged by order, by line or by case.

Definitions also move. [Walmart began fining suppliers for late and for early deliveries in 2017](https://www.mhlnews.com/global-supply-chain/article/22054435/walmart-suppliers-will-be-fined-for-both-early-and-late-deliveries), [asked for 87% in 2019](https://www.supplychaindive.com/news/walmart-on-time-in-full-87-suppliers/550083/), and [raised the target to 98% in 2020](https://talkbusiness.net/?p=153006).

## What the scorecard said

[Store 014](../data/) takes a truck from its company's warehouse almost every morning. For June's 25 deliveries, [the warehouse's report said 92% were on time and in full, and the store's scorecard said 88%](../logistics/#five-readings-of-one-delivery). [The delivery on 22 June counted as perfect](../logistics/#five-readings-of-one-delivery).

## What happened

At 03:00 on 22 June, [a picker scanned the label on the 5 kg atta slot and took 16 bales of 1 kg packs from the slot next to it](../logistics/#what-actually-happened). [The truck arrived at 06:12, inside its slot](../logistics/#monday-612-in-the-morning), and [the store booked 64 bags of 5 kg that did not exist](../logistics/#what-actually-happened).

Because the world is simulated, we know what was on every pallet: [84% of June's deliveries arrived on time with every case what the store ordered](../logistics/technical/#otif-reconciled).

## Why the number missed it

Every record about the delivery was written from one event, the pick confirmation: [the shipping notice copied it, the pallet label pointed to the notice, and the goods receipt copied the notice](../logistics/#why-the-data-could-not-say-so). That is the industry's design: [a pallet label's SSCC identifies the pallet and points to the shipping notice](https://gs1uk.org/support-training/help-to-use-and-implement-gs1-standards/logistics-labels-and-ssccs), and [Carrefour Belgium describes scanning the SSCC of every incoming pallet](https://www.gs1.org/docs/casestudies/7-_Case_Study_Carrefour_Belgium.pdf).

"In full" is a fact about the cases on a pallet, and [none of June's 30 pallets was weighed or case-scanned](../who-saw-what/#who-saw-what). So [all ten of the store's data tests on the shipping records passed](../logistics/technical/#tests-that-pass): records copied from one source always agree.

## How to catch it

Add an observation that does not come from the pick. [Scanning every case at the store would have found the 1 kg bales that morning](../logistics/#what-would-have-helped).

Without new scans, compare receipts with the next count. [One receipt in June did not survive the count that followed it, and taking that delivery out of the scorecard brings OTIF from the records to 84%](../logistics/technical/#otif-reconciled), the answer key's number.

## If an AI agent reads it

Asked at the supplier review whether the 22 June delivery was on time and in full, [the series' scripted AI assistant, given the shipping schema, said yes](../logistics/technical/#the-assistant-with-and-without-a-domain-layer). Given the warehouse's corrections, [the same assistant said on time but not in full, and named the line](../logistics/technical/#the-assistant-with-and-without-a-domain-layer).

## Try it on your own data

Ask which record says "in full" in your OTIF, and what would have to happen for that record to disagree with the shipping notice. If the answer is nothing, your OTIF measures paperwork.
