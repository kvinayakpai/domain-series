In my previous blogs, I followed a supermarket that exists only in a simulation, Store 014, and learnt that every KPI has a blind spot. Retail was familiar ground for me, as my first data warehousing project had me coding KPIs for a CPG business. So does the lesson hold in a domain I know very little about?

Oil and gas is that domain for me. Recently I also asked AI to build me a visual learning guide to the industry.

Who decides how many barrels a well produced? Why is a refinery's margin not the price everyone quotes? What does a meter see, and what does it miss? I believe the answer is the same as in retail – knowing what a record cannot see is domain knowledge. This season is my attempt to test that belief, not just state it.

## Field 014: my second attempt at an answer

Here is where AI takes over (this is AI talking).

To test it, I built an oil field and a refinery. Both exist only in a simulation. Field 014 is an onshore field in western India with sixteen wells, each lifted by an electric submersible pump, and its oil is piped to Refinery 014 on India's west coast. I followed them through March 2026, a hard month for the industry. At the end of February, conflict in the Gulf cut tanker traffic through the Strait of Hormuz. Before it, about [20.9 million barrels of oil a day went through the Strait, around a fifth of the world's oil use](https://www.eia.gov/international/content/analysis/special_topics/World_Oil_Transit_Chokepoints). By the second quarter of 2026, [the flow was 4.9 million](https://www.eia.gov/outlooks/steo/report/energysecurity/article.php). About [52% of India's crude imports pass through the Strait](https://www.spglobal.com/energy/en/news-research/latest-news/crude-oil/030526-india-weighs-alternate-crudes-refining-costs-economic-fallout-as-iran-conflict-drags-on), and within days India's refiners were talking to other suppliers.

Because I built the simulation, I know what really happened, so I can check each record against reality. Field 014 keeps the records a real field keeps: a fiscal meter, monthly well tests, a production allocation report, the pumps' daily data and an event log.

Let me start with one well. On Wednesday 18 March, the production report credited Well 14, the field's best well, with 803 barrels. It produced 542, because its pump had been wearing for two weeks. Its last test was 40 days old when the pump stopped on 20 March, and the field's meter was right all month. The report was computed correctly, and it was wrong about Well 14.

The pipe and the refinery have blind spots of their own, which later blogs measure: a meter that reads a little high every day and still passes every check, and a refinery that runs nearly full while it earns less than the price screens suggest.

**Knowing what a record cannot see is domain knowledge.**

## Why it matters more now

A person who reads a wrong KPI might question it. An AI agent acts on it, and agents are arriving in this industry fast. As per the 2026 Powering Possible survey of almost 400 energy leaders by ADNOC, XRG and Microsoft, [the share deploying integrated agentic AI workflows more than doubled in a year, from 15% to 33%](https://hydrocarbonprocessing.com/news/2026/10/investments-start-to-pay-off-as-energy-industry-accelerates-ai-adoption-but-infrastructure-must-keep-pace/). Meanwhile PetroBench, a 2026 benchmark of 1,200 petroleum engineering questions, found [the best of eight language models scoring around 72–74%, weakest in reservoir engineering](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.28032). Taken together, I read these as a caution: agents are moving into operations faster than they are learning the domain.

## How this season works

The season has 23 short blogs, one idea each.

1. **This blog.**
2. **Eighteen KPI blogs**, one KPI each, from the well, the pipe and ship, and the refinery. Each answers the same four questions: what is the KPI, what did it say at Field 014 or Refinery 014, what could it not see, and how do you catch it. Each ends with one rule to give an AI agent.
3. **Four linking blogs**, because the eighteen KPIs are not eighteen separate problems.

Every Field 014 number comes from its records, which anyone can download from the series' site, and every real-world fact names its source.
