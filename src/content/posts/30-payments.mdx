## Saturday, 6:48 in the evening

It is the Saturday evening rush, and the queues at Store 014 are long. A customer's basket comes to ₹1,064.47. She scans the QR code at the till, approves the payment on her phone, and her bank takes the money.

The till waits 30 seconds, hears nothing back, and shows "Payment failed: no response". The cashier asks for another way to pay, and she pays by card.

87 seconds after she approved the UPI payment, the store's bank confirms the credit. Nobody at the till sees it. She phones the store on Tuesday, and gets ₹1,064.47 back on the following Sunday, eight days after her shopping.

## Five readings of one payment

Nobody here is careless. Each data product reads the records in front of it.

- **The payments dashboard.** The tills approved 95.9% of June's UPI attempts. On Saturday 20 June approvals fell to 69% and 67% in the 18:00 and 19:00 hours, and between 18:20 and 19:50 cashiers asked for another payment 30 times. The acquiring bank's switch was slow, and the incident was closed.
- **The data tests.** All ten pass, because every bill has exactly one approved payment, and the settlements add up.
- **The reconciliation report.** On Saturday there were 607 UPI credits against 597 approved payments, and on Sunday 670 against 663. The 17 credits with no approved payment behind them go into a suspense account until someone claims them.
- **Customer support.** 26 customers complain about payments in June. The routine is to ask for a screenshot, check it against the provider's report, and refund.
- **The AI assistant.** Given the till's schema, the series' scripted assistant says nobody paid twice, and calls the store's 0.76% technical decline rate acceptable.

## What actually happened

On Saturday evening the acquiring bank's UPI switch slowed down, and between 18:20 and 19:50 about a third of UPI attempts at Store 014 got no answer within the till's 30 seconds. Put differently, the tills gave up on 28 of 82.

Now here is the part many of us miss. A slow answer is not a failed payment. [When the customer has been debited and the answer is late, UPI settles the payment afterwards: the merchant's bank either confirms the credit or the money goes back](https://www.npci.org.in/PDF/npci/upi/circular/2017/Circular-17-Debit-Reversals-and-Deemed-Approval_0.pdf). [Since 2019, RBI has required that money taken for a merchant payment that is never confirmed comes back within five days, with ₹100 a day in compensation after that](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/NotificationUser.aspx?Id=11693).

Across June, 24 customers paid twice: 26 extra charges, ₹13,366.06, the rest of them on ordinary days. The store refunded a double payment only when the customer complained, and when June closed four extra charges, ₹2,846.06, were still sitting in suspense.

**The till said failed, the bank said paid, and the customer paid twice.**

## Why the data could not say so

A till that hears nothing back never learns the payment's reference number, the RRN, so there is nothing to join on. The provider's report has the RRN and a success, and the till has a failure and no RRN.

Every record is right about what it saw, and the data tests check that the records agree, which they do. The only record of the double charge was in the customer's bank statement.

I believe this is not rare. [UPI processed 24.07 billion transactions in September 2026](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-processes-2407-billion-transactions-worth-rs-2937-lakh-crore-in-september), and [on 12 April 2025 its success rate fell to about 50% for nearly two hours](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-outage-attributed-to-surge-in-api-calls-from-banks-says-npci).

## What domain knowledge changes in the data work

Each change uses only the records, and each is scored against the answer key.

- **Treat a timeout as a question.** Matching each of June's 48 timeouts to the provider row with the same amount, started within five seconds, matches every one: 26 were credited late, 6 were debited and returned, and 16 never left the customer's account.
- **Then the double charges are a query.** It finds all 26 extra charges, with no false alarms. Run each morning on the provider's file, it finds them a median 0.6 days after the debit, against 2 days for complaints, and three customers never complained.
- **Measure the wait from the debit.** From the complaint, refunds took a median 3 days, from the extra debit 4.9, and 10 of the 22 refunds took longer than five days.
- **Read rates by the hour, with timeouts counted.** June's technical decline rate, 0.76%, counts no timeouts at all, and in the 18:00 and 19:00 hours on 20 June the tills got no answer on 25% and 26% of attempts.
- **Give the assistant a domain layer.** With definitions, the provider's report and two views built from them, the same assistant answers seven questions of seven, against one with the till's schema.

## What would have helped

The demo tries two changes from 1 June, with the same customers, baskets and banks.

- **Wait for the status check.** [NPCI's rules allow the first check on a payment's status 90 seconds after it](https://www.medianama.com/2025/04/223-npci-new-guidelines-upi-outage/). If the till waits for it before asking for another payment, double charges in June fall from 24 customers to 9, but during the slowdown the tills spend 99 minutes taking payment instead of 77.
- **Put the bill ID on every UPI request.** Each credit then names its bill, and the next morning's match finds and refunds every extra charge: the median wait falls from 4.9 days to 0.7, nothing is left in suspense, and complaints fall from 26 to 12.

The cheaper fix is in the back office, and not at the till.

**A payment that carries a reference to what it is for can always be matched to it.**
