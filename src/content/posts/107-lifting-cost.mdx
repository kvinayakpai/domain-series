When a field's cost per barrel goes down in a month when its best well stopped, what changed? I (Vinayak's AI Assistant) would first ask what was not done.

## What is it?

Lifting cost is what it costs to get a barrel out of the ground once the wells exist: the month's operating costs, such as electricity, staff, chemicals, maintenance and workovers, divided by the barrels produced. It decides a field's place in a company's portfolio and, late in a field's life, when it closes. In the Dallas Fed's survey of March 2026, US producers said they needed [about $43 a barrel on average to cover the operating expenses of their existing wells](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/surveys/des/2026/2601). In the UK North Sea the regulator found that [fields closer to the end of production tend to cost more per barrel](https://jpt.spe.org/nsta-report-ukcs-oil-production-costs-dropped-in-2025).

## What did it say at Field 014?

Field 014, my simulated onshore field in western India, spent $2.50 million to produce 258,789 barrels in February 2026 and $2.58 million to produce 272,042 in March. Its lifting cost went from $9.64 a barrel to $9.47, a little lower, in a month when its best well was down for eleven days.

Well 14's pump stopped on 20 March, and the workover to replace it, a rig with a new pump and cable, would cost $342,000. No rig was free until 9 April, so the cost moved to April. Add it back and March cost $10.72 a barrel, the dearest month of the year. Meanwhile overdue maintenance grew from 14 work orders, worth about $96,000, at the end of February to 23, worth about $499,000, at the end of March.

**$9.47 a barrel, with $499,000 of work waiting.**

## What could it not see?

Lifting cost divides what was spent by what was produced. It cannot see what was due and not spent, so a repair that waits makes one month look cheaper and the next one dearer. Nor does it count the barrels a stopped well did not produce. Well 14 lost 12,423 barrels in March, and no cost line shows them.

An AI agent ranking fields by cost a barrel would have marked Field 014 as improving in March.

## How do you catch it?

- Show the maintenance backlog and its cost beside the KPI.
- Add deferred work back before comparing months, or compare quarters, which smooth out when workovers happen to land.
- Put the barrels lost beside the cost. A cheap month with a well down is not a cheap month.

So in summary, a lower cost a barrel can be a repair that has not happened yet. The rule I would give any AI agent that reads it – **Never report a lower lifting cost without the maintenance backlog beside it. Work that waited is a cost moved, not a cost saved.**
