Can a good month hide a bad hour? Store 014 says yes.

## What is it?

The technical decline rate is the share of payment attempts that fail for technical reasons, such as a bank or switch error, rather than for the customer's reasons, such as a low balance. Teams that manage payment providers watch it to hold banks and switches to their service levels. As per Inc42, [NPCI's chief executive put UPI's technical declines at 0.7–0.8% of transactions in November 2024](https://inc42.com/buzz/technical-glitches-on-upi-have-fallen-below-1).

## What did it say at Store 014?

Store 014 is a supermarket in Jayanagar, Bengaluru, that exists only in a simulation I built, along with its tills and its bank's UPI switch. I followed every bill paid in June 2026.

The store's technical decline rate for June was 0.76% of UPI attempts, in line with UPI as a whole. By that number the month was healthy.

On Saturday 20 June, between 18:20 and 19:50, the tills got no answer within 30 seconds on 28 of 82 UPI attempts, about a third. None of them counts as a technical decline.

**A month at 0.76%, and an evening when a third of attempts got no answer.**

## What could it not see?

It missed in two ways. Timeouts are not counted as technical declines at all. And a month averages away an hour: in the 18:00 and 19:00 hours that Saturday, the tills got no answer on 25% and 26% of attempts.

Slowdowns across the whole system happen too. [On 12 April 2025, UPI's success rate fell to about 50% for nearly two hours](https://www.outlookmoney.com/banking/upi-outage-attributed-to-surge-in-api-calls-from-banks-says-npci).

## How do you catch it?

- Count timeouts as failures, and read the rate by the hour. Flagging any hour with three or more timeouts and a share more than ten times the month's picks out exactly two hours in June: 18:00 and 19:00 on 20 June.
- Watch the worst hour of the month, and not only the average.
- Track the switch's response time alongside the decline rate.

So in summary, a monthly rate can be perfectly healthy while one evening is not. The rule I would give any AI agent that reads it – **Read failure rates by the hour, with timeouts counted, before you trust a monthly rate.**
