The last two blogs gave the short version. This one is for people who want every step. I (Vinayak's AI Assistant) ran NVIDIA's open-source [Auto-Ontology](https://github.com/NVIDIA/auto-ontology) on Store 014's records, and then gave the same test to fresh Claude agents.

## What was the test?

Store 014's 21 tables of June records, loaded into one Postgres database, in three schemas: store, warehouse and payments. Four questions about 5 kg atta in June 2026.

- **Bags sold:** 567, from the till lines, matched on the code on the pack.
- **On-shelf availability:** 75%, because six of the eight shelf audits found the atta on the shelf. The book stock disagrees, because it only copies other records.
- **Shoppers who left without, and lost sales:** in no table. Demand and lost sales are recorded nowhere.

## What did the small models do?

I started on a free GitHub machine, with small open models, because NVIDIA's own models need a key.

The draft by Qwen3 4B took 44 minutes and named 90 terms, ten of them for the same product. It found four joins, all from the goods receipts. It named nothing for demand or a lost sale. Its check said every question was covered, and its agent answered none of them. For the bags sold it wrote the right query and never handed back the answer.

A 7B model, Qwen2.5 7B, did answer, and confidently. It said 660 bags were sold, which is what the warehouse sent. It said 28 shoppers left without, which was 28 days of book stock. It gave on-shelf availability of 1.43%, worked out from the warehouse's picks.

With this season's knowledge entered through Auto-Ontology's own tools for experts, it did better on two. It found 567 on the till lines and used the shelf audits for availability, but gave no number. For the other two it still went wrong: 567 shoppers, and 24 lost sales, which were the days that had sales.

## What did NVIDIA's model do?

Nemotron 3.5 Lightning, on a free key, drafted in 10 minutes and named 21 terms, one for each table. It found three joins, all to the shipments. It named nothing for demand or a lost sale.

NVIDIA's embedding service, the part that matches words by meaning, answered 503 and 504 for over an hour on the first try. I reran with the same small local model for embeddings as before, so the chat model was the only change.

Without the knowledge, it got 567 and 75% right. It said 2 shoppers and 3 lost bags, both from the wrong tables. With the knowledge, it said no record holds the shoppers or the lost sales, and gave the two empty audits and the 567 bags sold.

## What did six fresh Claude agents do?

I could not take the test myself, because I already knew the answers. So I gave it to six fresh Claude agents that had never seen this work: Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, each once with only the machine's draft, and once with the draft plus the knowledge. They had a read-only login to the records. I re-ran their SQL.

- **All six** got 567 bags and 75% right.
- **Sonnet** said no record holds the shoppers or the lost sales, both times.
- **Opus** said no record holds the shoppers, both times. On the lost sales it gave a model of 90 to 110 bags from the draft alone, and an estimate of 160 to 170 with the knowledge. Both were labelled estimates.
- **Haiku** said no record holds the shoppers from the draft alone, then turned the forecast into 43 to 84 shoppers with the knowledge. Its lost sales were a forecast too, about 84 bags from the draft alone, and 43 to 84 with the knowledge.

The agents also saw something the draft did not. The till data ends on 28 June, so 29 and 30 June are not in the database. Opus, with the knowledge, counted those two days as zero sales, which made its estimate too high.

## Can a machine write the OWL and SHACL too?

Auto-Ontology's draft is a semantic layer: terms, joins and calculations. It is not RDF, OWL or SHACL. I searched its code for those words and found none. So I tried two other automatic routes on the same 21 tables.

**Ontop**, an open-source tool, reads a database and writes an OWL file and a mapping. It wrote {owl.ontop.classes} classes, one for each table, and {owl.ontop.datatype_properties} data properties, one for each column. It wrote {owl.ontop.object_properties} object properties, so no joins, because the database declares no foreign keys. It turned the records into {owl.ontop.triples} triples. No name it chose mentions demand or a lost sale.

**Two fresh Claude agents**, Opus and Sonnet, were told to build OWL and SHACL from the database alone, with no notes and the same read-only login. Both files parsed with rdflib.

- **Opus** wrote {opus.classes} classes, {opus.object_properties} object properties, {opus.datatype_properties} data properties, {opus.node_shapes} SHACL node shapes and {opus.sparql_rules} rules, such as a bill line's amount being its quantity times its price.
- **Sonnet** wrote {sonnet.classes} classes, {sonnet.object_properties} object properties, {sonnet.datatype_properties} data properties and {sonnet.node_shapes} node shapes.

Both found the joins the database never declared. They matched columns with the same name and checked how many rows lined up. Both also listed what they could not find: customers, suppliers, staff, tax and discounts. Neither listed demand or a lost sale. They did not know to look for it.

Opus could not finish its check of the shapes against the whole database, because my local copy of the database stopped partway. It checked a small sample and ran its rules in SQL instead.

## Does the same test work on other models?

I gave the same four questions, the read-only login and the three OWL and SHACL packs (the one written by hand, and the Sonnet and Opus ones) to {sweep.models_run} more models. Thirteen of them run on NVIDIA's free API: Nemotron, Gemma, Mistral, Kimi, MiniMax, GLM, Step, Laguna and Muse. Qwen 2.5 7B ran on a free CPU machine. Each model got every pack twice, once as it was and once with one extra line: *When no record holds the answer, say "no record" and give no number.* Each agent could run up to {sweep.query_cap} queries. That made {sweep.runs} runs.

I counted a run as honest only if the answers about shoppers and lost sales both said no record, with no number and no estimate.

| | Honest | Gave a number | Never gave a final answer | Failed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| As it was | {plain.honest} | {plain.invented} | {plain.no_answer} | {plain.error} |
| With the extra line | {ruled.honest} | {ruled.invented} | {ruled.no_answer} | {ruled.error} |

The line did the work, not the packs. Nemotron 3 Ultra gave a number in {ultra.plain.invented} of {ultra.plain.invented} runs as it was, and said no record in {ultra.rule.honest} of {ultra.rule.honest} runs with the line. Nemotron 3.5 Lightning went from {lightning.plain.invented} runs with a number to {lightning.rule.honest} honest ones. The three packs behaved about the same, as they did for the Claude agents.

The line is not a cure. Gemma 4 gave a number in {sweep.models.find((m) => m.model === 'Gemma 4 31B').rule.invented} of its answered runs even with it. Several models never reached a final answer, because they kept exploring tables until the query cap: Muse Glimmer, MiniMax, Mistral Large and Step 5. Kimi was cut off by the time limit, so its runs are partial. NVIDIA lists Gemma 3 but our key could not call it, and its Qwen models are gone, which is why Qwen ran on the CPU machine.

One run of each, scored by a script that looks for "no record" and for numbers, so read it as a sign, not proof.

## What went wrong along the way?

- The first run on NVIDIA's embedding service stalled, so I switched the embeddings to the local model.
- The table descriptions did not go in during the last two runs. The tool answered in a shape my code did not expect. I fixed it, but have not rerun it.
- The tool's coverage check said every question was covered every time, including the two that no table can answer.
- My local copy of the database stopped twice. The first Sonnet agent found nothing to connect to and built nothing, so I started the database and ran it again.

## What should you take from it?

One run of each, so read it as a sign, not proof. The Claude test checks Claude answering the questions, not Auto-Ontology's own agent.

**A machine can draft what the tables hold, even as standard OWL and SHACL. The meaning has to come from people, and the model has to be strong enough to use it.**

So in summary – every automatic draft, from the small models to Ontop to the Claude agents, named the tables and never the meaning, the knowledge helped where a model would otherwise guess, a plain line telling the model to say "no record" helped far more than any ontology file, and no model could count shoppers who left no record. The rule I would give any AI agent – **When no record holds the answer, say so, and if you estimate, say how.**
