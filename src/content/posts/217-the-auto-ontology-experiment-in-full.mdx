## What was the test?

Store 014's 21 tables of June records – Season 1's published record files, without the answer keys – loaded into one Postgres database in three schemas: store, warehouse and payments. Column types were read from the values, a primary key was set only where rows are unique, and there were no foreign keys and no descriptions.

Four questions about 5 kg atta (ATTA-WW-5KG) in June 2026, and the true answers:

- **Bags sold:** 567, from the till lines (pos_lines), matched on the code on the pack. The daily sales table gives the same.
- **On-shelf availability:** 75%, because six of the eight shelf audits found the atta on the shelf, and two – 12 and 26 June – found it empty. The book stock disagrees, because it only copies other records.
- **Shoppers who left without, and lost sales:** in no table. Demand and lost sales are recorded nowhere.

The till data ends on 28 June, so 29 and 30 June are not in the database.

Nearly a thousand words of plain English, generated from the season's ontology by auto-ontology-demo/expert_layer.py, plus a glossary, calculations and certified queries. It went into Auto-Ontology through the tool's own channels for experts:

Rule 3's "Here:" line is the one the hard questions turn on: *If asked how many shoppers left without an item, or for lost sales, do not count goods receipts, shelf audits, forecasts or write-offs as shoppers or sales. Say that no record holds them, and if you estimate them, say how.* That is targeted. So this test checks whether a model follows written knowledge, not whether it can find the gap on its own – which is the point. The gap is what a person has to write down.

## What were the three harnesses?

1. **Auto-Ontology's own agent** (runs 4 to 10). NVIDIA's Auto-Ontology at commit 43fa2d3 (19 November 2026), run with Docker Compose on GitHub's free machine, with 4 CPUs and 16 GB. A small adapter served Ollama models for chat, nomic-embed-text embeddings padded to the 2048 dimensions its pgvector tables expect, and reranking by cosine similarity. Its ReAct agent answers through a submit tool, and a coverage check says whether the ontology covers a question.
2. **Fresh Claude agents.** Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, each with a read-only login to the records, the four questions, and either Nemotron's 21-term draft alone or the draft plus the knowledge layer. Their SQL was re-run on the records. This tests Claude answering, not Auto-Ontology's agent. A second test, on 23 November, gave the same three models the Sonnet and Opus packs instead.
3. **The sweep harness.** A text-to-SQL loop: one of three OWL and SHACL packs pasted into the prompt as Turtle ("Use it as you see fit"), a read-only SQL tool, temperature {sweep.prompt.temperature}, and up to 30 turns – 16 in the earlier batches, which covers {rev.turn_caps['16']} of the {sweep.runs} runs. The base prompt already said: *{sweep.prompt.base_line}*

The sweep is the same idea run many times, one GitHub job per model, so jobs run side by side (up to 10 at a time):

<Picture src="images/the-auto-ontology-experiment-in-full/sweep.png" width={1200} height={3600} narrow alt="Whiteboard: the sweep. ## What did the small open models do?

Run 6 used Qwen3 4B, at 4 bits, for drafting and answering. The draft took 44 minutes and named 90 terms, with 136 column attributes. It named the product ten times, once for each table that holds a SKU. It found four joins, all from the goods receipts – to the shipment, the pallet and the product. A fifth, from units_booked, was thrown out by its own check against the data. It found no join between the till's payments and UPI's report, and merged complaints and refunds, by RRN, into one "Customer Transaction". It suggested no calculations: only the Snowflake connector reads past queries, and this was Postgres. Apache Ossie refused the export, because goods_receipts.asn_sku mapped to two terms, Goods Receipt Line and Goods Receipt Note.

No term was named for demand or a lost sale. Demand came up only inside two descriptions, of how much a forecast can vary and of the reorder point. One description called the book stock "the final count of stock" for the day.

The coverage check said every question was covered, including the two no table can answer. The agent answered none of them. For the bags sold it ran the right query – 567 from the daily sales – but answered in plain text and never called submit, so the loop ended after two nudges. An earlier run, asked about "June" without the year, searched the wrong year, and counted the shelf audits that found the bay empty as shoppers.

Runs 7 and 8 kept the 4B draft and put Qwen2.5 7B on the answers.

- **Draft alone:** one run said 660 bags sold – SUM(units_booked) from the goods receipts – and gave on-shelf availability as "11 units received", which was 11 receipt rows. The other got 567 from the daily sales, gave 28 shoppers who left without – COUNT(*) of the book-stock rows, one a day – and on-shelf availability of 1.43%, AVG(cases_confirmed) over SUM(cases_ordered) from the warehouse's picks.
- **With the knowledge layer:** 567 from pos_lines joined to products on the GTIN. On-shelf availability as the share of shelf audits with status 'on shelf', but with no SQL and no number. The shoppers who left without were answered with the 567 bags sold. "Estimated lost sales 24" came from a CTE named no_sales_days that in fact listed the days with sales – 24 of the 28.
- **Coverage:** every question covered, before and after. After, it at least matched the right things.

## What did NVIDIA's own model do?

Run 9 used NVIDIA's models for everything, as Auto-Ontology ships. The embedding endpoint answered 503 and 504 for over an hour, and the ingest gave up after four attempts, so the run says little about the tool.

Run 10 used Nemotron 3.5 Lightning (nvidia/nemotron-3.5-lightning-30b-a3b) for drafting and answering, with the same local embeddings as before, so the chat model was the only change. It drafted in 10 minutes and named 21 terms, one for each table, with 135 column attributes and no calculations. No term or description named demand or a lost sale. It found three joins, all to warehouse.shipments.shipment_id – from goods_receipts, pallets and pick_confirmations – rejected customer_complaints.bill_id and upi_refunds.bill_id, and never considered a join between the till's payments and UPI's report. The Ossie export went through.

- **Draft alone:** 567 from the daily sales and 75% from the shelf audits, both right. The shoppers who left without became 2 – the shelf audits with status 'empty' – and the lost sales became 3, the bags written off as damaged.
- **With the knowledge layer:** both right answers kept. For the shoppers: "No table records shoppers who left without the item", with the two empty audits and the 567 bags sold. For the lost sales: "not stored in any table per the organization's rules", with the same two figures.
- **Coverage:** every question covered, before and after. Every answer's SQL re-ran on a local copy of the records with the same numbers.

Run 13 repeated run 10 after the fix, so that the table descriptions went in – 20 of the 21 tables took one, certified. The draft again named 21 terms, and the coverage check again said every question was covered, including the two no table can answer.

- **Draft alone:** 567 and 75%, both right. The shoppers who left without became 13,291, the 13,851 shoppers of the month less the 560 who bought the item, and the lost sales became 3, the write-offs again.
- **With the knowledge layer and the descriptions:** 567 and 75% again. For the shoppers it said no record holds them, and gave the 2 empty audits and 560 till lines. For the lost sales it gave an estimate of 142 bags, the sales times the share of empty audits, which is 25%. It said how, as rule 3 allows, but run 10 had said "not stored in any table" for the same question.

So the descriptions went in, and I cannot say they helped. It is one run of each, and the lost sales answer went the other way.


## What did six fresh Claude agents do?

First, how the Claude agents were run. They ran on my computer, not on GitHub: each got the questions, some notes and a read-only login to Postgres, and I re-ran their SQL to score them.

I could not take the test myself, because I already knew the answers. So I gave it to six fresh Claude agents that had never seen this work: Haiku, Sonnet and Opus, each once with only Nemotron's draft, and once with the draft plus the knowledge layer.

- **All six** got 567 bags and 75% right.
- **Sonnet** said no record holds the shoppers or the lost sales, both times.
- **Opus** said no record holds the shoppers, both times. On the lost sales it gave a model of 90 to 110 bags from the draft alone, and an estimate of 160 to 170 with the knowledge layer, both labelled as estimates.
- **Haiku** said no record holds the shoppers from the draft alone, then turned the forecast into 43 to 84 shoppers with the knowledge layer. Its lost sales were a forecast too: about 84 bags from the draft alone, and 43 to 84 with the knowledge layer.

Opus, with the knowledge layer, counted 29 and 30 June – not in the data – as days with no sales, which pushed its estimate up.

## Did the Sonnet and Opus packs change what the Claude agents did?

The six agents above never saw the OWL and SHACL packs that Sonnet and Opus had written. So on 23 November I gave the same three models those packs. Each model answered the four questions for each pack, once with the base prompt and once with the added line *When no record holds the answer, say 'no record' and give no number.* That is 12 runs, one each. The agents read the Turtle files from a folder instead of having them pasted into the prompt, and had a read-only login to a local copy of Store 014's records. I re-ran their SQL: 567 bags and 6 of 8 audits on shelf, as before.

- **All 12** got 567 bags and 75% right. Most called the 75% a sample of 8 spot checks, not the month's availability.
- **Nobody turned the forecast into shoppers.** All 12 said no record holds them.
- **Sonnet and Opus** said no record for the lost sales in all 8 runs, with and without the line. One Sonnet run added that about 20 bags a day sold on typical days, and one Opus run a rough 80 to 100 bags, both as context and not as an answer.
- **Haiku** said no record for the lost sales when it had the line. Without it, it still gave an estimate from the forecast: about 84 bags with the Opus pack, and 42.6 units of forecast demand on the two empty-audit days, about ₹11,289, with the Sonnet pack. Both were labelled estimates, so it kept to no record in 6 of its 8 answers on the two questions, and Sonnet and Opus in all 8 of theirs.

So with these packs Haiku stopped turning the forecast into shoppers in all four of its runs, where it had done so once with my hand-written layer. It still estimated the lost sales unless told not to. This is one run each, the packs hold different knowledge from my layer, and the agents read files where the sweep pasted them, so I cannot say which of these made the difference.

One Sonnet run, with the Opus pack and the line, read only the first 1,143 of the 1,459 lines of the shapes file. I ran it again with an instruction to read to the end. The first rerun found my local database stopped, so I started it and ran that agent once more. The answers were the same as before.

## Can a machine write the OWL and SHACL too?

<Picture src="images/the-auto-ontology-experiment-in-full/routes.png" width={1200} height={3194} narrow alt="Whiteboard: three machine routes from the same 21 tables. Auto-Ontology wrote a semantic layer of 21 terms with Nemotron and 90 with Qwen3 4B, not RDF, OWL or SHACL. Ontop wrote an OWL file of 21 classes and 140 data properties with 0 links. The Opus and Sonnet agents wrote OWL and SHACL with 33 and 30 classes and 32 and 27 shapes. None of the three named demand or a lost sale, because no table holds them." />

Auto-Ontology's draft is a semantic layer – terms, joins and calculations – not RDF, OWL or SHACL. A search of its code found none of those words. So I tried two other automatic routes on the same 21 tables.

**Ontop**, an open-source tool, reads a database and writes an OWL file and a mapping. It wrote {owl.ontop.classes} classes, one for each table, and {owl.ontop.datatype_properties} data properties, one for each column. It wrote {owl.ontop.object_properties} object properties, so no joins, because the database declares no foreign keys. It turned the records into {owl.ontop.triples} triples. No name it chose mentions demand or a lost sale.

**Two fresh Claude agents**, Opus and Sonnet, were told to build OWL and SHACL from the database alone, with no notes and the same read-only login. Both files parsed with rdflib.

- **Opus** wrote {opus.classes} classes, {opus.object_properties} object properties, {opus.datatype_properties} data properties, {opus.node_shapes} SHACL node shapes and {opus.sparql_rules} rules, such as a bill line's amount being its quantity times its price.
- **Sonnet** wrote {sonnet.classes} classes, {sonnet.object_properties} object properties, {sonnet.datatype_properties} data properties and {sonnet.node_shapes} node shapes.

Both found the joins the database never declared, by matching columns with the same name and checking how many rows lined up. Both listed what they could not find: customers, suppliers, staff, tax and discounts. Neither listed demand or a lost sale. They did not know to look for it.

Opus went further than any draft in places. It described the book stock as "{owl.opus_noticed.book_stock}", it matched the {owl.opus_noticed.timeouts_matched_to_upi_report} till attempts that ended in 'failed: no response' to UPI's report by equal amount and a start time within 5 seconds, and it noted that the only forecast method in the data is a "{owl.opus_noticed.forecast_method_seen}" – a forecast of sales, not of demand. A strong model can find what the data shows. It cannot find what the data never held.

Opus could not finish checking its shapes against the whole database, because my local copy of the database stopped partway. It checked a small sample and ran its rules in SQL instead. Sonnet named the till lines "{owl.sonnet_named_till_lines}", which is fine for people – but in the sweep below, a small model went looking for a bill_lines table that does not exist.

## Does the same test work on other models?

<Picture src="images/the-auto-ontology-experiment-in-full/results.png" width={1200} height={3008} narrow alt="Whiteboard: tries with all four answers right across 14 models and 82 tries. I gave the same four questions, the read-only login and the three OWL and SHACL packs – the one written by hand, and the Sonnet and Opus ones – to {sweep.models_run} models. Thirteen ran on NVIDIA's free API, with the model writing its SQL in a fenced block, and Qwen2.5 7B ran on a free CPU machine. Each pack went in once as it was and once with one extra line: *{sweep.prompt.extra_line}* That made {sweep.runs} runs.

A script scored them first, looking at questions 2 and 4 for the words "no record" and for digits. It found {auto.plain.honest} honest runs as they were, and {auto.rule.honest} with the line. Reading every answer changed that picture in three ways.

1. **It counted refusals in other words as numbers.** Nemotron 3 Ultra's "Cannot be determined from available data" and Nemotron 3.5 Lightning's "Cannot be determined" and "Not directly computable" scored as numbers, and so did a Lightning answer that cited "Rule 3".
2. **It looked only at questions 2 and 4.** Runs that said "no record" to every question – even the 567 bags sold – counted as honest, and so did runs that gave 296, 326 or 0 bags sold.
3. **It counted one "none" as honest,** though that run had run no query, said its queries returned nothing, and so claimed no shoppers and no lost sales.

So I counted a run as all four right only if it gave 567 bags and 75%, and said that no record holds the shoppers and the lost sales, with no number or estimate for them.

## Who offers the tools, and what do they write?

<Picture src="images/the-auto-ontology-experiment-in-full/market.png" width={1200} height={2322} narrow alt="Whiteboard: who offers the tools and what they write. A machine drafts: Ontop and Fabric IQ in OWL; NVIDIA Auto-Ontology, Snowflake and Databricks Genie in their own format. People model it: Protégé, Stardog and Timbr with OWL; Palantir Foundry and Apache Ossie in their own format. None can draft what no table holds, so people still write it." />

A **formal ontology** is a file in open standards – RDF, OWL and SHACL – that a computer can check and any tool that speaks the standard can read. A **context layer** is a map of business meaning for an AI – definitions, metrics, joins, rules in words. It is useful, but a machine cannot prove anything from it. Most products are the second kind, even when they say ontology.

## What should you take from it?

1. Every automatic route – Auto-Ontology on two models, Ontop, and Claude writing OWL and SHACL – named the tables and never named demand or a lost sale.
2. Plain-language knowledge tied to the records moved where models looked. It turned NVIDIA's model from confident guesses into honest answers.
3. The season's knowledge as formal OWL and SHACL files – with no line tying a rule to a table – did no better than packs a machine wrote from the tables, and misled some small models into querying tables that do not exist.
4. One plain behavioral line did more than any pack: {rev.plain.all_four_right} runs all four right without it, {rev.rule.all_four_right} with it. Repeating the Opus and Sonnet packs six times each gave the same answer: the line mattered, the pack did not.
5. The model matters: some answered without a query, some refused everything, some never finished. Score all four answers, not just the hard ones.

**A machine can draft what the tables hold, even as standard OWL and SHACL. The meaning has to come from people, in words the model can use, and the model has to be strong enough to use them.**

So in summary – one run of each setting, three different harnesses and a hand-read score, so read it as a sign, not proof: the knowledge that changed the answers was written by a person, in plain words, tied to the real records. The rule I would give any AI agent – **Test the knowledge layer the way you test code – with questions whose answers you already know, including "no record".**
