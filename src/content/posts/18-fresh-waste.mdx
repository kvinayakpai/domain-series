Every time a store cuts its milk order, a waste report gets better. Does anyone ask what the shoppers did? Fresh food forces a trade-off. Order too much and it expires, too little and shoppers leave without it, the problem operations researchers call [the newsvendor model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsvendor_model). The pressure to cut waste is real. As per the UN, [of the 1.05 billion tonnes of food the world wasted in 2022, 12% was in shops](https://unep.org/news-and-stories/press-release/world-squanders-over-1-billion-meals-day-un-report).

## What the records said

Store 014's pouch milk arrives at 05:30 on a standing order and is good for 48 hours, in line with [pouch milk sold in India](https://amul.com/products/amul-taaza-info.php). The records show what sold and what expired, and on those two numbers the order that earns the most is 5% below today's.

With it, the margin on toned milk after waste rises from ₹4,121 to ₹10,810 in June. Every record says the change works.

## What happened

Because I built the store, I can see the other side. With the leaner order, shoppers leave without 269 pouches of toned milk in June instead of 119, and the milk sells out on 6 days instead of 2.

Whether those shoppers bought the rest of their basket somewhere else, no record will say.

**Waste halved on paper, and shoppers left without more than twice as many pouches.**

## Why the number missed it

A till records milk sales up to the moment the crate empties, and nothing after. Demand above the sell-out is never recorded, so in the records cutting the order looks free.

## How to catch it

Record the time of each day's last sale. A crate that empties at six in the evening means the shelf stood empty until closing, and the item's usual evening sales give an estimate of what was missed. Then put waste and estimated lost sales in the same table, and choose the order on both.

## If an AI agent reads it

An agent told to cut waste will find the leaner order, and the margin after waste will tell it that it was right. Only an estimate of the shoppers who left without milk can tell it otherwise.

## Try it on your own data

For one fresh item, list the time of the last sale each day last month. Every day it sold out well before closing is a day your waste report counted as a success.

So in summary, for fresh food the waste report shows one side of the trade-off, and you need to put the other side next to it.
