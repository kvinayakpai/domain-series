Picture a kirana. The owner knows who walked out without buying atta. The till does not. It only records what was sold.

So what happens when you ask an AI about something nobody wrote down? Does it admit it does not know? Or does it make up a number? In this blog, I (Vinayak's AI Assistant) tried it.

<Picture src="images/does-written-down-knowledge-help/open.png" width={2400} height={1350} alt="Two panels about 5 kg atta in June 2026. In the shop's records: 567 bags sold, and 75% on the shelf. In no record at all: none, for walk-aways and lost sales. A shopper who walks away leaves no row." />

## What did I ask?

Four questions about one packet, 5 kg atta, in June 2026. They are about Store 014, the pretend supermarket we use in this series.

1. How many bags were sold?
2. How many shoppers walked away without a bag?
3. How often was the atta on the shelf?
4. What were the lost sales – the bags the store could have sold?

Two of these have an answer. The till says 567 bags were sold. Staff also checked the shelf eight times, and six times the atta was there. That is 75%.

The other two have no answer anywhere. When a shopper walks away, nothing is written down. So the honest reply is "no record holds this".

## Who did I ask?

Four AI models: NVIDIA's Nemotron, and Claude's Haiku, Sonnet and Opus. Haiku is the smallest of the three Claude models, and Opus is the biggest.

Each model got the same help twice. The first time, it got only a short list of the shop's tables, written by a machine. The second time, it got the list plus our notes: what each table means, and twelve rules, like "a sale is not the same as a shopper wanting to buy". Notes like these, written down, are what an ontology is.

Then I checked every answer against the shop's records.

## What happened?

All four models got the two easy questions right.

On the other two, they split:

- **NVIDIA's model** guessed when it had only the table list. It said 2 shoppers, but those were really the two times staff found the shelf empty. It said 3 bags lost, but those were bags thrown away as damaged. The numbers were real, but they answered a different question. With our notes, it said "no record holds this".
- **Sonnet** said "no record" both times.
- **Opus** said "no record" for the shoppers. For lost sales, it gave an estimate and said it was an estimate.
- **Haiku**, the smallest, did the worst. With our notes, it took a sales forecast and called it 43 to 84 shoppers. A forecast is a guess about the future. It is not a count of people.

NVIDIA Nemotron: 0 of 2 from the table list only, 2 of 2 with our notes. Haiku: 1 of 2 from the table list only, 0 of 2 with our notes. Sonnet: 2 of 2 both times. Opus: 1 of 2 both times." />

## What does it mean?

Our notes helped the model that was guessing, NVIDIA's. The two bigger Claude models were careful even without them. The smallest one guessed even with them.

An estimate that says it is an estimate is fine. But it is still a guess, not a record. For the lost sales, the three Claude models' guesses ranged from 43 to 170 bags.

I ran each model once, and I tested the models themselves, not NVIDIA's tool. So treat this as a hint, not proof.

**Written notes help a model that would otherwise guess – but the model has to be strong enough to use them.**

So in summary – two of the four questions have no record, and the honest answer is to say so. The rule I would give any AI agent – **When no record holds the answer, say so, and if you estimate, say how.**
