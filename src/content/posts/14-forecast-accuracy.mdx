Have you ever scored a forecast against sales and felt good about the result? Forecast accuracy compares forecasts with what actually happened, with measures such as [mean error, which shows bias, and mean absolute percentage error](https://pkg.robjhyndman.com/forecast/reference/accuracy.default.html). What actually happened usually means sales. As an example, [the M5 competition, one of the largest forecasting benchmarks, asked teams to forecast Walmart's unit sales](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v38y2022i4p1346-1364.html).

## What the scorecard said

Store 014 forecasts each item as the 28-day average of its recorded sales. Scored against what the tills recorded, its forecast for 5 kg atta looks 13% too high over June, so the obvious tuning would lower it.

## What happened

Because I built the store, I know how many shoppers came for a bag, and against them the same forecast was 8% too low.

The recorded sales were missing 39 open hours with an empty shelf in mid-June and 51.5 more after a wrong pallet. The forecast learnt the gaps, and the score rewarded it for that.

**Scored on sales, the forecast looked 13% too high. Scored on shoppers, it was 8% too low.**

## Why the number missed it

The forecast learns from sales, and its score is computed against sales. When the shelf is empty, sales measure the shelf, not the shoppers, so the training data and the test data have the same hole.

That makes the score point the wrong way. A forecast built on estimated demand looks 22% too high against recorded sales, worse than the store's own, although it is closer to what shoppers wanted. A team that scores on sales would throw away the better forecast.

## How to catch it

Score against estimated demand wherever the shelf was empty. Filling the empty hours with the sales the item usually makes and scoring against that shows the store's forecast running too low, and the demand-based forecast only 1% too low over June.

It also helps to look at bias separately in the weeks that had long no-sale runs. If a forecast looks too high mainly in those weeks, the scorecard is measuring the shelf.

## If an AI agent reads it

An agent that tunes forecasts on recorded sales will push this one down. The scripted replenishment agent in the store's rerun of June did. It saw three days of sales at less than half the forecast and cut it from 23.8 bags a day to 20.6.
