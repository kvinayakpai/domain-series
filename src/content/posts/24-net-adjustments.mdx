Is a net number always a safe number? Net inventory adjustments add up a period's stock corrections, gains against losses, and large errors can cancel. As per the US General Accounting Office, [a special inventory at the US Navy's supply centers in 1982 found gains worth $239 million and losses worth $200 million](https://www.gao.gov/assets/nsiad-84-9.pdf), a net of $39 million out of $439 million in corrections.

## What the report said

Across the warehouse and the store, June's four atta corrections net to ₹80 at cost. By that number, the inventory was accurate.

## What happened

One mis-pick caused all four. In the warehouse, the 1 kg slot came up 16 cases short and the 5 kg slot 16 cases over. At the store, a count found 63 bags of 5 kg missing and 157 more 1 kg packs than the books showed.

Summed without netting, the four corrections come to ₹44,648.

**₹80 net, and ₹44,648 gross, from one mistake.**

## Why the number missed it

The mistake moved stock from one item to its sibling, and from one building to the other, so the corrections cancel each other when they are added up. A net close to zero says nothing about the size of the mistakes inside it.

And no one looked across them. Each building found its own two corrections, days apart, and closed them on its own.

## How to catch it

Report gross corrections beside the net, and pair them. June's records hold five mirror pairs. Four join the atta corrections, and the fifth is the two milks at the month-end count. The pairing rule is a few lines of SQL.

## Try it on your own data

Put last quarter's gross adjustments beside the net, by building. If gross is many times the net, pair the corrections before anyone calls the inventory accurate.

So in summary, a net near zero can hide a lot of gross error, so always ask for both.
