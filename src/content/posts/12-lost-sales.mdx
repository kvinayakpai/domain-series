A lost sale is a sale a store would have made if the product had been on the shelf, and [shoppers do walk away](https://www.fmi.org/newsroom/news-archive/view/2002/10/14/new-industry-study-explores-root-causes-of-global-out-of-stock-dilemma): in a worldwide study of grocery out-of-stocks, 31% said they go to another store when an item is missing, 26% buy another brand, and 9% buy nothing at all.

## What the records said

At [Store 014](../data/), the fictional Bengaluru supermarket this series simulates, [no table records a lost sale](../retail/technical/#the-records): the tills record what was scanned, and a shopper who leaves scans nothing.

Asked how many bags of 5 kg atta shoppers wanted but did not get in the week of 8 June, [the series' scripted AI assistant, given only the schema, answered "None recorded"](../retail/technical/#the-assistant-with-and-without-a-domain-layer).

## What happened

Because the store is simulated, we know every shopper who came for a bag: in June, [shoppers left without 157 bags of 5 kg atta](../retail/#now-let-an-agent-act-on-it).

Most of them came during two gaps: [71 bags while fourteen cases sat behind the rice](../retail/#what-actually-happened), and [72 after a pallet labelled 5 kg arrived holding 1 kg packs](../logistics/#what-actually-happened). Of those 72, [29 became trips to another store, 16 took a different pack size, 12 took another brand, 9 came back later, and 6 were never bought](../logistics/#what-actually-happened).

## Why the number missed it

The usual shortcut multiplies out-of-stock days by average sales, but [the books showed stock all through the first gap](../retail/#what-actually-happened) and [until the count in the second](../logistics/#what-actually-happened), so the records show almost no out-of-stock time to multiply.

Substitutes hide part of the loss inside other sales, and shoppers who come back later look like ordinary sales. [Economists who tracked stock in 54 vending machines found that ignoring stock-outs biases demand estimates](https://www.nber.org/papers/w14315), and understates how much stock-outs cost.

## How to catch it

Estimate it from the hours the shelf was probably empty. [The store's gap detector](../retail/technical/#finding-the-censored-hours) finds runs of no sales that the item's usual rate cannot explain while the books show stock.

[Filling those hours with the sales the item usually makes](../retail/technical/#unconstraining-demand) puts June's demand for 5 kg atta at 716 bags, against 717 asked for in the answer key. For the wrong pallet alone, [the same method puts the loss at about 82 bags](../logistics/technical/#the-shelf-side); the answer key says 72.

An estimate is a model, not a record, so report it with its method and the hours it covers.

## If an AI agent reads it

An agent that reads lost sales as zero has nothing to fix. Given the hours the shelf was probably empty, [the same scripted assistant answered "about 68" for the week of 8 June](../retail/technical/#the-assistant-with-and-without-a-domain-layer); the answer key says 71.

## Try it on your own data

Take your best-selling item. Find the longest run of hours last month with no sales while the system showed stock, and multiply it by the item's usual hourly sales. That is a first estimate of a loss no report of yours will show.
