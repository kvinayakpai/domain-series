Ask five companies what OTIF means and you may get five answers.

## What is it?

On time, in full is the share of deliveries that arrive when promised with everything ordered. As per McKinsey, [there is no standard definition of it](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/operations/our-insights/defining-on-time-in-full-in-the-consumer-sector): companies differ on the time window, and on whether "in full" is judged by order, by line or by case.

Supply chain heads live by it, and suppliers pay for it. As per ShipCalm's 2026 guide, [Walmart's target is commonly 98% for most categories, and a miss costs roughly 3% of the cost of goods on the affected part of the order](https://www.shipcalm.com/blog/walmart-vendor-compliance-guide/).

## What did it say at Store 014?

Store 014 is a supermarket in Jayanagar, Bengaluru, that exists only in a simulation I built, along with the warehouse that supplies its staples. I followed eleven everyday items through June 2026, hour by hour.

For June's 25 deliveries, the warehouse's report said 92% were on time and in full, and the store's own scorecard said 88%. The 22 June delivery counted as perfect.

Checking every case against the order, 84% of June's deliveries arrived on time with everything the store ordered. The 22 June delivery was not one of them.

**The warehouse reported 92%, and the cases said 84%.**

## What could it not see?

Every record about a delivery was written from one event, the pick confirmation. The shipping notice copied it, the pallet label pointed to the notice, and the goods receipt copied the notice. That is how the industry is designed: [a pallet label's SSCC identifies the pallet and points to its shipping notice](https://gs1uk.org/support-training/help-to-use-and-implement-gs1-standards/logistics-labels-and-ssccs).

So "in full" meant the label said so. None of June's 30 pallets was weighed or case-scanned, and all ten data tests on the shipping records passed, because records copied from one source always agree.

## How do you catch it?

- Add one observation that does not come from the pick. Scanning every case at the store would have found the 1 kg bales that morning.
- Compare receipts with the next count. One receipt in June did not survive the count after it, and taking that delivery out of the scorecard brings OTIF to 84%.
- Ask, for every OTIF you are shown, which record says "in full".

So in summary, OTIF is only as independent as the records behind its "in full". The rule I would give any AI agent that reads it – **OTIF from documents measures paperwork. Ask how “in full” was verified before you act on it.**
