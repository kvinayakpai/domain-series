When a count comes up short, what is the first word that comes to mind? For most of us it is theft. Shrink is stock a retailer paid for and cannot account for: theft, damage and mistakes. As per the NRF survey reported by Retail Dive, [US retailers put it at 1.6% of sales in 2022, $112.1 billion](https://www.retaildive.com/news/retail-shrink-theft-changed-little-in-2022-nrf/694844/), and attributed 27% of it to process and control failures and errors rather than theft.

## What the loss report said

[Store 014's month-end count took 55 pouches off toned milk and added 48 to full-cream](../demo/#world-title). Read on its own, toned milk is losing stock, and [a data test had already warned that the full-cream book went below zero on six days](../retail/#five-readings-of-one-week).

Atta looked worse. [A count on 26 June found the books at 63 bags of 5 kg and the bay empty: ₹14,364 at cost, gone](../logistics/#four-corrections-one-mistake). Either the counting was sloppy, or bags were walking out of the store.

## What happened

Neither was theft. The milk was [the till's scan errors: pouches of one milk scanned as the other](../logistics/#what-domain-knowledge-changes-in-the-data-work), so one book drifted down while the other drifted up.

The 5 kg atta was never in the store. [A pallet line labelled 5 kg had held 1 kg packs](../logistics/#what-actually-happened), and [the same count found 157 more 1 kg packs than the books showed](../logistics/#five-readings-of-one-delivery).

**A shortage in one place was a surplus in another, and nobody stole anything.**

## Why the number missed it

A count adjustment records that the book was wrong, and not why. [The stock-count table holds the count and the adjustment](../retail/technical/#the-records), and theft, damage and a scan error all produce the same row.

Read one at a time, [the warehouse booked its two atta corrections as a short pick and a cycle count variance, and the store booked a loss](../logistics/#four-corrections-one-mistake).

## How to catch it

Pair adjustments before you explain them. A loss on one item and a gain of about the same size on its sibling is a scan or picking error, and not a thief.

[The pair rule in the logistics companion](../logistics/technical/#mirror-pairs) looks for corrections in one product family with opposite signs and similar sizes within a week. It finds five pairs in June, four from the wrong pallet and one for the two milks.

## Try it on your own data

Take last month's ten biggest count losses. For each, look for a gain of about the same size on a similar item in the same count. Every pair you find is a process to fix, and not a thief to chase.

So in summary, an adjustment tells you that the book was wrong. It takes a second record to tell you why.
