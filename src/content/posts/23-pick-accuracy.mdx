Every pick confirmed by a scan. Sounds like 100% accuracy, does it not? Pick accuracy is the share of orders a warehouse picks without error, and as per WERC's 2025 benchmark, [best in class is 99.68% or better](https://www.yale.com/globalassets/coms/yale/north-america/documents/white-papers/snack-drawer/YALE-1520_WERC-DC-Metrics-infographic.pdf). Even at that rate, about one order in 300 is picked wrong.

## What the records said

[All 70 of the warehouse's picks for Store 014 in June were confirmed by scanning the slot's label](../who-saw-what/#who-saw-what). By the records, pick accuracy was 100%.

## What happened

At 03:00 on 22 June, [the picker went to slot A-07-03 for 16 cases of 5 kg atta, scanned its label, and took 16 bales of 1 kg packs from slot A-07-04 next to it](../logistics/#what-actually-happened).

The warehouse noticed twice without knowing it. [Its 1 kg slot came up 16 cases short on Tuesday, and its 5 kg slot 16 cases over on Thursday](../logistics/#five-readings-of-one-delivery). [It closed each correction on its own](../logistics/#four-corrections-one-mistake).

**The warehouse found its own mistake twice, and never saw it was one mistake.**

## Why the number missed it

[A slot scan confirms where the picker stood, not what the picker took](../logistics/#what-actually-happened). Every record downstream copied that confirmation, so [the shipping notice, the pallet label and the store's receipt all agreed with it](../logistics/#why-the-data-could-not-say-so).

## How to catch it

Look for mirror images. [Two corrections in neighboring slots, with opposite signs and about the same size, within a week, are one mis-pick](../logistics/technical/#mirror-pairs).

Then follow the pair to the pick. [A query for 16-case picks of 5 kg from that slot in the week before returns one row, line 1 of the 22 June shipment](../logistics/technical/#the-trace). [The warehouse's pair was complete on Thursday 25 June, a day before the store's count](../logistics/#what-domain-knowledge-changes-in-the-data-work).

Or stop it at the source. [Scanning the barcode on each bale at pick would have rejected the 1 kg bale at 03:00](../logistics/#what-would-have-helped).

So in summary, a scan that confirms the place is not a scan that confirms the product.
