## The key that is not there

A UPI payment the till gave up on, "failed: no response", never learned its RRN. All {m.timeouts} of June's timeouts have a blank reference, so the obvious join returns nothing. The provider's report has a row for each of them all the same: the same amount, started in the same second. Match the timeouts to the provider rows no till payment claims, one to one, nearest in time first:

```sql
CREATE VIEW v_timeouts_matched AS
WITH orphans AS (
  SELECT * FROM psp_upi WHERE rrn NOT IN (SELECT rrn FROM till_payments WHERE rrn IS NOT NULL)),
pairs AS (
  SELECT t.bill_id, t.attempt, t.ts, t.amount_inr, o.rrn, o.status, o.status_ts,
         row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY t.bill_id, t.attempt
                            ORDER BY abs(epoch(o.initiated_ts) - epoch(t.ts))) AS k,
         row_number() OVER (PARTITION BY o.rrn
                            ORDER BY abs(epoch(o.initiated_ts) - epoch(t.ts))) AS j
  FROM till_payments t JOIN orphans o
    ON o.amount_inr = t.amount_inr AND abs(epoch(o.initiated_ts) - epoch(t.ts)) <= 5
  WHERE t.till_status = 'failed: no response')
SELECT *, CASE status WHEN 'success' THEN 'late credit' WHEN 'returned' THEN 'debited and returned'
                      ELSE 'never debited' END AS outcome
FROM pairs WHERE k = 1 AND j = 1;
```

All {m.matched} match, each within a second, and every outcome is the one the answer key records: {m.outcomes['late credit']} late credits, {m.outcomes['debited and returned']} debited and returned, {m.outcomes['never debited']} never debited. The five-second window matters: customers who retried by UPI made a second attempt for the same amount 30 seconds later, and a looser window would pair the wrong rows. Exercise one asks you to see it happen.

## The extra charges

A late credit on a bill the customer then paid again is an extra charge:

```sql
CREATE VIEW v_extra_charges AS
SELECT m.bill_id, m.rrn, m.amount_inr, m.ts AS debited_ts, m.status_ts AS credited_ts, a.tender AS paid_again_by
FROM v_timeouts_matched m
JOIN till_payments a ON a.bill_id = m.bill_id AND a.till_status = 'approved'
WHERE m.outcome = 'late credit';
```

It returns {m.extra_charges} rows on {m.customers} bills. Scored against the answer key: {m.true_positives} right, {m.false_positives} false alarms, {m.missed} missed.

## Clocks that start at the harm

Support measures a refund from the complaint. The customer lost the money at the debit. Measured from the debit, with refunds counted once they are done by the month-end review, the median wait is {f.refund_median_days_from_debit} days, {f.not_refunded} extra charges were still unrefunded, and {f.over_tat} of the refunds took longer than the five days RBI allows banks for a failed payment. The domain test uses that as the store's own clock; RBI's rule covers failed payments, not double charges, and nothing obliged the store to keep it.

## Rates by the hour

The month's technical decline rate counts failures the switch reported. Timeouts are not in it, and a month averages away an hour. Count timeouts, read by the hour, and flag an hour with three or more timeouts and a share more than ten times the month's ({(ai.hours.month_share * 100).toFixed(2)}%): two hours are flagged, 18:00 and 19:00 on Saturday 20 June.

## How to learn this domain, for data people

A rough plan for a few evenings a week over about two months:

1. **How UPI moves money.** Learn the parties in a payment: the customer's app and its bank, NPCI's switch, the customer's bank that debits, the merchant's bank that credits, and the provider that serves the merchant. Then read NPCI's circulars on how a payment that times out is settled afterwards, starting with its 2017 note on debit reversals and deemed approval.
2. **Cards, for contrast.** Read Glenbrook's *Payments Systems in the U.S.* for authorisation, clearing and settlement in the card networks and the four-party model. India's systems differ in detail, and the vocabulary is the same.
3. **When payments fail.** Read RBI's 2019 circular on turnaround times and compensation for failed transactions, and NPCI's 2025 circulars on status checks and response times. Note which party owes what, and by when.
4. **Reconciliation.** Learn the keys that join payment records: the RRN, the merchant's own transaction reference, settlement dates. Practise finding credits with no sale behind them and sales with no credit.
5. **Practice.** Work through the exercises above on [June's payment records](../../data/#payments-records) before you open the answer key.
