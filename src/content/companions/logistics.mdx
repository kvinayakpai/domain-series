## Corrections, in both buildings

The domain tests start by putting every correction in one place, in units and in cases. A mis-pick moves whole cases, and the two atta sizes come in different cases: four bags of 5 kg, ten packs of 1 kg.

```sql
CREATE VIEW v_corrections AS
SELECT 'warehouse' AS building, adjusted_ts AS ts, sku, split_part(sku, '-', 1) AS family, slot,
       units, CAST(cases AS DOUBLE) AS cases, value_at_cost_inr AS value_inr
FROM dc_adjustments
UNION ALL
SELECT 'store', count_ts, sku, split_part(sku, '-', 1), NULL,
       adjustment, adjustment / CAST(case_units AS DOUBLE), round(adjustment * cost_inr, 2)
FROM stock_counts JOIN products USING (sku)
WHERE adjustment <> 0 AND count_ts > TIMESTAMP '2026-06-01 07:00';
```

Netted, the atta corrections come to ₹{ai.mirror.net_atta_value_inr} at cost. Summed as they are, gross, they come to ₹{ai.mirror.gross_atta_value_inr.toLocaleString('en-IN')}.

## Mirror pairs

Two corrections mirror each other when they are in one product family, have opposite signs, are each at least a case, are within 15% of each other in cases, and fall within seven days. In one building they must be neighbouring slots at the warehouse, or two items at the store; across buildings, the same item.

```sql
CREATE VIEW v_mirror_pairs AS
SELECT a.*, b.*
FROM v_corrections a JOIN v_corrections b
  ON a.family = b.family AND sign(a.units) = -sign(b.units)
 AND abs(a.cases) >= 1 AND abs(b.cases) >= 1
 AND abs(abs(a.cases) - abs(b.cases)) <= 0.15 * greatest(abs(a.cases), abs(b.cases))
 AND abs(epoch(b.ts) - epoch(a.ts)) <= 7 * 86400
 AND (a.ts < b.ts OR (a.ts = b.ts AND a.sku < b.sku))
WHERE (a.building = b.building AND a.sku <> b.sku
       AND (a.building = 'store' OR neighbouring(a.slot, b.slot)))
   OR (a.building <> b.building AND a.sku = b.sku);
```

`neighbouring` compares aisle and bay and asks for positions one apart; the analysis script spells it out. The thresholds are choices, and exercise two asks what changes when you loosen them.

## The trace

A warehouse pair names two slots: the one that came up over is the slot the picker should have taken from, and the one that came up short is where the cases came from. So look for picks of the over item, from its slot, in the cases the pair moved, in the week before the first correction:

```sql
SELECT p.pick_ts, p.shipment_id, p.line, p.cases_confirmed, p.confirmed_by, n.sscc, n.units
FROM picks p JOIN notice_lines n USING (shipment_id, line)
WHERE p.sku = 'ATTA-WW-5KG' AND p.slot = 'A-07-03' AND p.cases_confirmed = 16
  AND p.pick_ts BETWEEN TIMESTAMP '2026-06-23 14:20' - INTERVAL 7 DAY AND TIMESTAMP '2026-06-23 14:20';
```

It returns one row: {t.shipment_id}, pallet {t.sscc}, line {t.line}, {t.cases_confirmed} cases picked at {t.pick_ts.slice(11, 16)} on 22 June and confirmed by a {t.confirmed_by}. The answer key says that line held {t.actual_units} packs of 1 kg. The pair was complete when the warehouse booked its second correction, {ai.mirror.days_to_pair.toFixed(1)} days after the truck arrived; the store's count came {ai.mirror.days_to_store_count.toFixed(1)} days after, and never found the cause.

## Fill rate, by the notice and by the item

Fill rate asks what share of the cases ordered were shipped. From the records it is a join of the shipping notice to the store's orders:

```sql
WITH shipped AS (
  SELECT order_id, sku, sum(cases) AS cases FROM notice_lines GROUP BY ALL)
SELECT sum(s.cases) AS shipped, sum(o.cases) AS ordered
FROM store_orders o JOIN shipped s USING (order_id, sku);
```

It returns {ai.fill.shipped} cases shipped of {ai.fill.ordered} ordered, {(ai.fill.by_notice * 100).toFixed(1)}%: two lines went out short, a case of dal and two of sugar. The 1 June truck carried an order placed on 31 May, which is not in June's orders file, so it is left out.

The notice cannot say whether a case held the item ordered. The answer key's pallet contents can: {ai.fill.wrong_item_cases} of the cases shipped on 22 June held 1 kg packs on a 5 kg line. Counting only cases of the right item, the fill rate is {(ai.fill.right_item * 100).toFixed(1)}%.

## OTIF, reconciled

The four ways of measuring OTIF in the post come straight from the records, except the last, which needs the answer key. A data team can get close to it once a count has happened: a receipt booked by a label scan is a copy of the notice, and a count is an observation, so flag the latest receipt before a count when the count takes off half or more of what it booked.

```sql
WITH latest AS (
  SELECT c.count_ts, c.sku, c.adjustment, max(r.received_ts) AS received_ts
  FROM stock_counts c JOIN goods_receipts r
    ON r.sku_booked = c.sku AND r.received_ts < c.count_ts
   AND r.received_ts >= c.count_ts - INTERVAL 7 DAY
  WHERE c.adjustment < 0 GROUP BY ALL)
SELECT r.shipment_id, r.line, r.units_booked, l.count_ts, l.adjustment
FROM latest l JOIN goods_receipts r ON r.sku_booked = l.sku AND r.received_ts = l.received_ts
WHERE -l.adjustment >= 0.5 * r.units_booked;
```

One line fails, the 22 June line of 5 kg. Take its delivery out of the scorecard and OTIF from the records is {Math.round(ai.otif.ways.reconciled * 100)}%: {ai.otif.good.reconciled} of {ai.otif.out_of.reconciled}, the same as the answer key's count of deliveries whose cases reached the shelf as ordered. The other three computed ways match the answer key exactly.

## How to learn this domain, for data people

A rough plan for a few evenings a week over about two months:

1. **How a warehouse works.** Read Bartholdi and Hackman's *Warehouse & Distribution Science*, free to download from the authors, for receiving, slotting and order picking, and Gwynne Richards's *Warehouse Management* for day-to-day operations. If you can, walk a warehouse from the receiving dock to dispatch.
2. **Supply chain fundamentals.** MIT's Supply Chain Fundamentals (CTL.SC1x) covers demand, inventory and transport, with the maths and the industry practice.
3. **Documents and identifiers.** Learn the GS1 logistic label, the SSCC and the despatch advice, and how a receiver matches them. In India, add the GST documents that move with goods: an e-way bill is required before moving most consignments worth more than ₹50,000, including movements that are not sales, such as transfers between a company's own sites (rule 138 of the CGST Rules; states can set a different limit for movement within the state).
4. **Metrics.** OTIF, case fill, pick accuracy and dock-to-stock time. Read how Walmart's OTIF programme changed, and look at WERC's DC Measures for how warehouses benchmark themselves.
5. **Practice.** Work through the exercises above on [June's shipping records](../../data/#logistics-records) before you open the answer key.
